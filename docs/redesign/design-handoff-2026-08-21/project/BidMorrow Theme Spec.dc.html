BidMorrow · apps/web · theme layer

Theme spec v2

Scope: full visual redesign Modes: dark (default) + light Source: apps/web/src/styles.css Repo: cploutarchou/bidmorrow @ main

One stylesheet drives the whole product, so the theme is one decision surface. This spec replaces the translucent near-black-plus-teal-glow layer with an opaque, stepped surface system, a real typographic voice, an editorial figure treatment, and a decision scale that stops competing with the brand accent. Every value below is paste-ready for the :root block in styles.css.

01 — Direction

Six rules that fix the six complaints

Weak hierarchy

Opaque steps, not glass

Five named surfaces — sunken, canvas, panel, raised, overlay — each one step lighter than the last, each carrying a hairline. backdrop-filter and the .glass tints come out; depth becomes readable, printable and cheap.

Type scale + font stack

A voice, not the OS default

Newsreader carries headings and figures with editorial authority; IBM Plex Sans runs the interface; IBM Plex Mono sets every money, date and score value with tabular figures. Seven steps, no in-between sizes.

Buttons + controls

Three ranks, one geometry

Solid accent, outline, quiet — at two heights (36 / 30px) and one radius. Fields sit on sunken ground with a 1.5px inner-lit edge, so a form no longer reads as a stack of empty cards.

Generic cards

The tender card is the product

It gets its own anatomy: a score gutter, a deadline in mono, the fit chip flush left, score components as thin rules, and one risk line. Fit strength is expressed by the left gutter weight — not by tinting the whole card.

Contrast + a11y

Ink lifted, accent demoted

Muted ink moves from #8b94a7 to #a3adbb so secondary copy clears 4.5:1 on every surface. The bright teal stops being body text: it fills buttons and marks state, and a lighter step handles inline links.

Feels dated

Instrument, not dashboard

No radial washes, no glow shadows, no gradient CTAs, radii down to 6px. What's left is a serious reading surface for a bid/no-bid decision — plus a genuine light mode for procurement teams who work in daylight and print.

02 — Surfaces & hierarchy

Five steps, one hairline each

Rule: a container may sit one step above its parent, never two, and elevation is carried by the step plus --line-hair. Shadow is reserved for things that float over the page (menus, dialogs, toasts).

04 — Decision scale

Fit is one hue, risk is another

Today classification borrows the status greens and ambers, so "worth reviewing" and "requires certification" shout with the same voice. New rule: fit is a five-step ramp of the accent — strength shown by fill weight, never by hue change — and amber/red belong to risk flags and billing only.

Rules

  • Every figure a user compares — value, deadline, score, CPV code, invoice amount — sets in Plex Mono with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. This replaces the .num class's current system-mono fallback.
  • Newsreader is never bolded past 500, and never used below 17px — it is a reading face, not a UI face.
  • Newsreader italic carries product-truth caveats (unknown-policy notes, disclaimers) so they read as editorial voice rather than error text.
  • Self-host all three families from apps/web/public/fonts with font-display: swap — the current CSP (style-src 'self') rules out a Google Fonts stylesheet.
  • Line length caps at 74ch for prose, 62ch for lede copy.

Step 1

Token swap + alias block

New :root and light block land, old names alias to new. Visual change: surfaces stop being translucent, ink lifts. No component edits.

Step 2

Type + controls

Self-host the three families, apply the seven-step scale, rebuild .cta/.btn-*/.form-field on the new geometry, add the theme toggle to Settings → Appearance.

Step 3

Card, feed, marketing

Tender card anatomy, score badges on the fit ramp, then the marketing washes and plan cards. Admin inherits with no bespoke work.

Open questions for you: keep teal as the action colour or move the accent to a colder cyan; whether light mode ships as a Settings preference or follows the OS only; and whether Newsreader also carries the marketing display or stays app-side.
