# Competitor findings — rendered-page analysis (2026-08-17)

Evidence-based profiles from full-page Playwright captures (CI run, branch
`competitor-shots`, desktop 1440×900 + mobile 390×844), analyzed by seven
specialist research agents per
`.claude/skills/website-redesign/templates/competitor-profile.md`.
External pages are untrusted research material; patterns are described,
never copied. Screenshots referenced by file name live on the scratch
branch `competitor-shots` under `raw/`.

## Convergent positioning themes

All seven analyses independently converge on five themes:

1. **Own the verdict, not the workflow.** Every direct competitor sells
   find-and-win breadth (search, monitors, pipelines, AI chat); none
   leads with the bid/no-bid decision itself. BidMorrow's sharpest
   truthful position is the qualification verdict for capacity-
   constrained 5–50-person EU IT/cyber consultancies.
2. **Determinism as trust.** The category says "AI-powered" and shows
   mascots; none explains scoring. BidMorrow's deterministic, LLM-free,
   component-inspectable engine is a verifiable counter-claim.
3. **Radical pricing honesty.** Competitors hide pricing behind sales
   calls or "from" anchors. Flat €29/€49 EUR on the homepage is a
   category-level differentiator (pricing substance frozen by owner).
4. **Provenance as a feature.** Nobody displays data-source attribution.
   The mandatory TED attribution and honest CPV coverage statement can
   be worn as trust, not buried as compliance.
5. **Conversion grammar worth learning (with original words):** one CTA
   verb everywhere; risk-reversal microcopy row under the CTA; product
   screenshots whose believability comes from data fidelity; deadline
   chips for scannability; a single sample tender traveling the page as
   a narrative spine.

## 1. Tendify

**Pages reviewed**: <https://www.tendify.eu/>, <https://www.tendify.eu/features/tender-pipeline>

**One-line read**: A polished, indigo-branded EU tender-intelligence SaaS that sells a "find →
monitor → pipeline → brief → AI chat" workflow as a team operating system, with
an unusually low-friction trial funnel (enter your company website, 7-day
trial, no card) — the strongest direct benchmark BidMorrow must differentiate
from rather than imitate.

### Positioning and messaging

Value prop is outcome-framed around winnability: the hero H1 is a short
confident claim of the form "know which public tenders you can win" (win-
focused, not search-focused). The supporting paragraph enumerates the workflow:
find relevant tenders, evaluate them with AI, monitor markets daily, understand
documents, manage opportunities in a structured pipeline. Audience targeting is
explicit and threefold, visible in the mega-menu: by workflow/feature (search,
monitors, pipeline, briefs, resources, AI chat), by role (sales & business
development, bid managers, founders & CEOs, procurement consultants, export &
market-expansion teams), and by industry (software companies, IT &
infrastructure, construction, healthcare & medical suppliers). Geographic
identity is a trust pillar: "Built in Europe, for European businesses" appears
in the nav coverage blurb and "Built in the European Union." in the footer.
Proof offered is thin: no customer logos, testimonials, or usage stats were
observable anywhere in either capture; the main credibility devices are a
realistic product UI screenshot with plausible tender data (contract values,
deadlines, contracting authorities, country flags), a lead-magnet report
positioned as market expertise ("EU software, IT & cybersecurity opportunity
report 2026" with a Get report CTA overlaid on the hero area), and community
signals (an "Office Hours" webinar announcement bar). Their AI assistant ("Riko
AI chat") is promoted as a named product character. Inference: they are pre-
social-proof stage, compensating with product-depth signaling and content
marketing.

### Sitemap and navigation

Top nav is lean: 4 items (Solutions, Industries, Resources — all dropdowns —
plus Pricing as a direct link) with a single pill CTA "Start your free trial"
at top right; logo left. The Solutions mega-menu is a large multi-column panel
organized "by feature" (tender search, daily monitors, tender pipeline, tender
briefs, Riko AI chat), "by role" (5 role pages), plus a Coverage entry and
About us, with an embedded promo card for the 2026 opportunity report. Above
the nav sits a full-width indigo announcement bar promoting the Office Hours
webinar. Footer is a heavy 4-column SEO/IA map: Features (6 links), For Your
Team (5 role links), For Your Markets (8 programmatic country pages — Germany,
UK, Poland, France, Croatia, Italy, Spain, Belgium), Resources (Blog, Coverage,
Glossary, Help center, Pricing, About us), plus tagline restated, "Built in the
European Union.", an anti-spam reassurance line, one LinkedIn icon, Privacy
Policy and Terms. Feature pages cross-link laterally: the pipeline page has a
6-step icon strip (Search → Monitor → Pipeline → Brief → Resources → Riko)
labeled as part of a "tender operating system", turning the feature IA into a
navigable product story.

### Homepage structure

Hero thesis first: announcement bar → nav → oversized H1 (two-tone: black text
with the final clause in brand indigo) stating the winnability promise → one-
paragraph workflow summary → a distinctive lead-capture combo (an input asking
for your company website URL + "Start your free trial" button) → risk-reversal
microcopy line ("Free 7-day trial • No credit card required • Set up your first
monitor on Day 1") → a large, detailed product screenshot of the Pipeline view
(list of live-looking tenders with countries, deadlines, "13d left" chips, an
open tender-detail panel showing contracting authority, contract value €34M,
languages, and an embedded "Chat with Riko AI" input). Below the hero, the page
continues with several tall sections that were captured blank because they are
scroll-reveal animated; what still rendered shows: a full-width indigo gradient
band (inference: a feature or CTA storytelling section), an embedded pricing
section with a Monthly/Annual billing segmented toggle directly on the
homepage, and the large footer. Total page height ~8,200px at 1440w — a long-
scroll single-narrative page. Order of arguments (observable + inferred):
promise → workflow → product proof → features → pricing → footer IA. CTAs:
identical "Start your free trial" in nav, hero, and (on the pipeline page)
after the subhead — one consistent conversion verb everywhere.

### Conversion journey

Single conversion path: free trial, repeated verbatim in the announcement-
adjacent nav pill, hero, and feature-page heroes. The hero form asks for the
company website URL rather than an email — inference: they auto-derive an
initial matching profile from the company site, which makes the first CTA feel
like starting the product, not signing up. Friction is explicitly dismantled in
microcopy: 7-day free trial, no credit card, and an activation promise ("set up
your first monitor on Day 1") that pre-sells time-to-value. Pricing is
transparent enough to sit on the homepage itself with a monthly/annual toggle
(plan cards were in a blank scroll-reveal region, so tiers/amounts not
observable in this capture). Secondary conversion paths: the gated 2026 EU
software/IT/cybersecurity opportunity report (lead magnet inside the mega-menu
and hero overlay) and the Office Hours webinar registration — a two-track
funnel (product trial + content capture). No demo-call CTA observed; this is a
self-serve PLG motion. A modal cookie-consent banner with
Accept/Reject/preferences interrupts the first impression on every page and
viewport.

### Visual identity and brand personality

Character: friendly-professional European SaaS, closer to Linear/Notion-era
polish than legacy tender portals. Palette: white/near-white ground with a fine
dot-grid texture, a single saturated indigo/violet brand color (used for the
announcement bar, CTA pills, headline highlights, footer accents, and full-
bleed gradient bands) plus soft lavender tints for chips and eyebrow labels;
small country-flag accents and amber/green status chips inside product mocks.
Typography: a modern grotesque sans throughout; very large hero display sizes
(multi-line, ~90-100px on desktop) with two-tone color emphasis — key phrases
of the headline set in indigo; uppercase letter-spaced eyebrow labels (e.g.
"TENDER PIPELINE") above feature-page H1s. Imagery is exclusively product UI —
high-fidelity fake-data screenshots and floating card fragments layered
behind/around the hero at low opacity, giving depth without photos or
illustration. Shape language: large border radii, pill buttons, soft diffuse
shadows, rounded cards. Density is airy with generous whitespace. Motion: not
directly capturable, but the fact that most mid-page sections rendered empty in
a fullPage screenshot proves aggressive scroll-triggered reveal animation
across nearly all below-fold content (observable side effect); the layered
translucent hero fragments suggest parallax/float effects (inference).

### Product presentation

Product is shown through detailed static UI recreations, not video or
interactive demos. The homepage hero embeds a full Pipeline screen: sidebar
(Monitoring, Pipeline), toolbar (search, Stage/Owner/Deadline filters,
List/Board toggle, "Customize stages"), summary stats ("12 tenders • €109.4M in
flight"), tender cards with country flags and deadline countdown chips, and an
opened detail panel exposing real-feeling fields (submission deadline,
location, contracting authority, accepted languages, tender type, contract
value, published date) plus an inline "Chat with Riko AI" prompt box. The
pipeline feature page repeats this with kanban-stage fragments ("Preparing
bid", "Submitted" with per-stage totals like €4.2M) and a task/actions
checklist inside a tender card ("Brief ready", a comment, a dated to-do) —
showing collaboration mechanics, not just data. The "tender operating system"
step strip (Search → Monitor → Pipeline → Brief → Resources → Riko) frames each
feature as one station in a pipeline-shaped product tour. No pricing
screenshots, no video player controls, no interactive embed observed. On mobile
the same hero screenshots shrink to unreadable thumbnail scale rather than
being recomposed.

### Mobile experience

At 390px the marketing narrative survives well: announcement bar wraps to two
lines, nav collapses to logo + hamburger, the hero headline recomposes to 3-4
large centered lines keeping the two-tone indigo emphasis, and the subhead
remains readable. Footer stacks all four link groups into a single centered
column with generous tap spacing. Feature-page eyebrow chip + giant headline
also scale cleanly. Weak points: the hero product screenshots are scaled down
whole — tender cards and detail panels become illegible ~6px text thumbnails
instead of a mobile-recomposed crop; the cookie modal covers most of the first
viewport on mobile and demands interaction before content; mobile pages are
extremely long (12,008px home) with the same blank scroll-reveal dependency;
the website-URL trial input was not visible above the cookie overlay in
capture, so its mobile ergonomics are unverified.

### Accessibility and usability observations

Observable positives: strong text contrast in headings/body (near-black on
white), large touch-target pill CTAs, visible form labels/placeholder in the
hero input, consistent link styling in footer, a Reject option of equal
prominence-ish in the cookie banner. Observable concerns: the low-opacity
layered hero fragments create text-over-text collisions (in capture, the mega-
menu/hero overlay text visibly interleaves with the H1 — partly a capture
artifact, but the ghosted background cards are by design and reduce hero
legibility); indigo-on-white link text in soft lavender tints (footer market
links, eyebrow chips) trends toward low contrast; near-total reliance on
scroll-triggered reveals means content is invisible without
JS/IntersectionObserver — if not paired with a reduced-motion/no-JS fallback
this is an accessibility and robustness failure (the blank fullPage captures
are direct evidence the content genuinely does not paint until scrolled);
countdown chips ("13d left") use color+text, which is fine, but status meaning
in kanban chips relies partly on color. Keyboard, focus-visible, and screen-
reader behavior are not assessable from screenshots.

### SEO and content strategy

Deliberate programmatic and content SEO, all observable from the IA: eight
"Tenders in {Country}" market landing pages in the footer (classic programmatic
geo-SEO for "tenders in germany"-type queries); five role landing pages; six
feature pages under a /features/ path (confirmed URL pattern: /features/tender-
pipeline); a Glossary (procurement-terminology long-tail), a Blog, a Coverage
page (data-source transparency doubling as SEO), and a Help center. Content-
marketing layer: a gated annual sector report (EU software/IT/cybersecurity
opportunities 2026) and recurring "Office Hours" webinars — both are demand-
capture assets aimed squarely at IT/cyber bidders, i.e. directly at BidMorrow's
ICP. Feature pages use keyword-bearing eyebrow labels and H1s with the feature
term in the first words. No public per-tender SEO pages observed in the
nav/footer (consistent with them monetizing search inside the app).

### Trust, security, privacy presentation

Trust is built on EU identity and anti-spam posture rather than compliance
badges: "Built in Europe, for European businesses" (nav) and "Built in the
European Union." (footer) are the loudest signals, plus "No spam, only tenders
relevant to your business." in the footer. GDPR surface: a full cookie-consent
modal with Accept/Reject/preferences and a linked Privacy notice on every page;
footer links Privacy Policy and Terms of Service; © 2026 Tendify. Nothing
observable on: data-source attribution (no visible TED/OJEU attribution
anywhere in the captures), security posture (no ISO/SOC/GDPR badge, no security
page link), company registration details, or team identity ("About us" exists
but content not captured). Only one social channel (LinkedIn). Notable gap for
a data product: no visible statement of where tender data comes from or how
fresh it is — the Coverage page presumably carries this, but it is not surfaced
on the homepage.

### Strengths

- Outcome-first headline strategy: the hero sells winnability (a judgment) rather
  than coverage (a database) — exactly the mental model BidMorrow also targets,
  and they occupy it with confidence.
- Company-website-as-signup-input hero form makes the trial feel like instant
  personalization and collapses onboarding into the first click; paired microcopy
  promises Day-1 activation (first monitor set up).
- Coherent product-as-system story: the
  Search→Monitor→Pipeline→Brief→Resources→Riko step strip turns six feature pages
  into one legible operating-system narrative with lateral navigation.
- Three-axis audience IA (feature / role / industry) plus eight programmatic
  country pages gives them wide SEO surface and lets every visitor self-select a
  tailored entry path.
- High-fidelity product screenshots with realistic EU tender data (contracting
  authorities, EUR contract values, deadline countdowns, country flags) do the
  proof work that missing testimonials cannot.
- Disciplined single brand color and single CTA verb repeated everywhere — low
  cognitive load, unmistakable conversion action.
- EU-native trust framing (built in the EU, no-spam promise) matched to the
  anxieties of European SMB buyers.
- Two-track funnel: self-serve trial plus content capture (annual sector report,
  Office Hours webinars) that nurtures not-yet-ready buyers — aimed directly at
  the IT/cybersecurity segment.

### Weaknesses

- Below-fold content is invisible without scroll-triggered JS animation: in full-
  page captures the majority of both pages rendered as blank white/indigo bands —
  evidence the content does not paint until IntersectionObserver fires, a
  robustness, reduced-motion, and potentially SEO/paint-performance liability.
- Zero social proof anywhere observable: no logos, testimonials, customer counts,
  or named users — the trust burden rests entirely on UI screenshots and EU
  framing.
- No visible data provenance: nothing on the homepage says where tenders come
  from, how complete coverage is, or how fresh the data is — a serious omission
  for a data product and an opening for a competitor that leads with transparent
  TED attribution and honest coverage statements.
- AI-led evaluation claim with no explanation: 'evaluate them with AI' and the
  Riko chat are asserted, never justified — no accuracy, methodology, or
  explainability story; buyers burned by AI hallucination have nothing to hold
  onto.
- Hero legibility is compromised by layered translucent UI fragments
  behind/through the headline; the ghosted collage aesthetic actively fights the
  H1.
- Cookie-consent modal dominates the first viewport (especially mobile), delaying
  the value proposition on every entry page.
- Mobile product screenshots are naive shrink-downs — the core proof asset is
  illegible exactly where a growing share of first visits happen.
- Broad multi-industry, multi-role positioning (construction, healthcare, sales
  teams, consultants) dilutes depth: no single ICP sees content that speaks its
  exact language on the homepage itself.
- Pricing tiers were not visible without scrolling animation; only the billing
  toggle rendered — pricing transparency exists but is buried deep in an 8,200px
  page.
- Long-scroll pages (8k-12k px) with heavy animation suggest a large JS/asset
  payload (inference from structure; not measurable in screenshots).

### Differentiation opportunities for BidMorrow

- Own the bid/no-bid decision, not the workflow suite: Tendify sells breadth
  (search+monitor+pipeline+briefs+AI chat). BidMorrow should sell one sharp
  verdict — a deterministic, explainable qualification score for IT/cyber
  consultancies — positioning Tendify's breadth as noise for a 5-50 person firm.
- Lead with provenance and honesty where they are silent: put TED attribution
  (Commission Decision 2011/833/EU), the decision-support disclaimer, honest CPV
  coverage, and data-freshness front and center as a trust feature; their
  homepage says nothing about data sources.
- Anti-AI-opacity positioning: they say 'evaluate with AI' with a chat mascot;
  BidMorrow can truthfully claim a deterministic, LLM-free scoring engine whose
  every component is inspectable — 'the same tender always gets the same score,
  and you can see why.' That is a genuine, checkable differentiator, and it
  aligns with the no-AI-framing content rule (stating LLM-free is fine).
- Narrow ICP as a weapon: their homepage speaks to six industries and five roles
  at once; BidMorrow's homepage can speak only to EU IT/cybersecurity
  consultancies of 5-50 people, with CPV codes, framework types, and
  certification barriers that audience actually recognizes — depth they
  structurally cannot match on a generalist homepage.
- Radical pricing simplicity: publish €29/€49 flat EUR immediately and near the
  top — against their buried, toggle-behind-animation pricing, visible flat
  pricing is a conversion and trust statement (presentation only; frozen content
  per standing decisions).
- Progressive-enhancement performance as brand: build the site to render fully
  without JS and honor prefers-reduced-motion — the exact failure their scroll-
  reveal architecture exhibits — and make instant-paint speed part of the macOS-
  inspired 'it just feels right' character.
- Recompose product proof for mobile instead of shrinking it: design mobile-
  specific crops of the score breakdown (one readable card, one readable verdict)
  where they show illegible thumbnails.
- Honest founding-stage proof: they hide having no customers; BidMorrow can
  convert the same weakness with the 'first 50' founding-cap honesty — scarcity
  plus candor instead of silence.
- Steal the activation promise mechanic, not its form: their 'first monitor on
  Day 1' microcopy works; BidMorrow's equivalent should be an original time-to-
  first-verdict promise expressed in its own onboarding-first UX (onboarding is
  the stated top UX priority), without copying the company-website-input pattern
  literally.

### Design lessons (patterns described, never copied)

- Two-tone headline emphasis: setting the pivotal phrase of a very large H1 in
  the single brand color creates hierarchy and brand recall without any
  decoration — cheap to implement in vanilla CSS and CSP-safe.
- One CTA verb everywhere: the identical trial CTA in announcement-adjacent nav,
  hero, and feature pages removes decision friction; adopt the discipline (single
  primary action per page) with original wording.
- Risk-reversal microcopy row directly under the primary CTA (trial length • no
  card • concrete Day-1 outcome) — a compact pattern that answers the three
  biggest objections in one line.
- Feature pages as stations on a visible pipeline: a small icon step-strip ('part
  of your …') on every feature page that shows where this feature sits in the
  overall flow and links laterally — excellent for comprehension and internal
  linking.
- Eyebrow label + giant statement H1 + one-paragraph subhead + CTA as the
  feature-page hero grammar — a clean, reusable template.
- Product screenshot as hero proof, rendered with realistic domain data (real-
  looking authorities, EUR values, deadline chips, flags) — believability comes
  from data fidelity, not UI chrome; BidMorrow should invest in a meticulous
  fake-but-plausible TED dataset for its own mocks.
- Deadline-countdown chips ('13d left') on tender cards: a tiny UI element that
  injects urgency and scannability into list views.
- Footer as sitemap: grouped columns by feature/role/market/resource make the
  whole IA legible in one place and carry the programmatic SEO pages.
- Dot-grid background texture and soft-shadowed rounded cards give an airy
  technical feel at near-zero bandwidth cost — compatible with a macOS-inspired
  layered-surface direction without copying anyone.
- Negative lesson: never gate content rendering behind scroll-triggered animation
  without a no-JS/reduced-motion fallback — their pages are provably blank in
  full-page rendering; BidMorrow should treat 'fully painted without JS' as a
  hard design constraint.
- Negative lesson: hero background collages of translucent UI fragments undermine
  headline legibility; keep proof imagery spatially separated from display type.

## 2. Stotles

**Pages reviewed**: <https://www.stotles.com/>, <https://www.stotles.com/pricing>

**One-line read**: Well-funded UK-centric "one B2G platform" that has moved upmarket from tender
alerts to an end-to-end public-sector sales suite (Sales Studio + Bid Studio)
with AI bid writing, a free tier as top-of-funnel, and sales-led pricing from
£99 to £475+/month.

### Positioning and messaging

Positions as a single end-to-end "B2G platform" for the entire public-sector
sales and bidding process, not a tender-alert tool. The hero headline uses a
rotating/completion pattern ("One B2G platform built to [do X]" with the
variable phrase in a contrasting accent color — the capture caught "create
target lists"). The secondary section headline is a punchy two-sentence
imperative pair about shaping the tender and winning the bid, with the second
clause in purple. Subhead frames the scope: from early market intelligence to
AI-assisted bid writing, everything in one place. Audience is explicitly multi-
role enterprise/mid-market sales and bid teams ("One B2G platform, built for
every role" — pipeline builders, bid writers, campaign runners), skewing much
larger than BidMorrow's 5-50-person consultancy ICP. Proof offered: an
enterprise logo bar (Palo Alto Networks, Iron Mountain, Elastic, Dun &
Bradstreet, AtkinsRealis, Appian) directly under the hero and repeated as
"TRUSTED BY" on pricing; no visible stats, testimonials, or review badges in
the captures. Notably, "bid/no-bid" appears only as a small feature step ("Go
straight from bid/no-bid to first draft" — the bid/no-bid report is framed as
the trigger for AI drafting), not as the core value proposition: qualification
is a means to their AI-writing end, which leaves the qualification-first
position open.

### Sitemap and navigation

Top bar: announcement banner (2026 Summer Release invite with underlined link)
above a two-tier header. Main nav has 5 items — three dropdowns (Products,
Solutions, Resources) plus two flat links (Data, Pricing) — then a secondary
outline CTA "Search for tenders" and a primary filled "Book a call"; Log in /
Sign up live in the thin banner strip, unusually de-emphasized. Footer is a
three-column sitemap: Platform (7 links mirroring the funnel: Pricing,
Overview, Create strategy, Build pipeline, Track tenders, Win bids, Data),
Resources (11 links incl. Industry reports, Blog, Meet buyers in person,
Partners, Competitors, Public sector glossary, CPV codes finder, Our data, Help
centre, Tender explorer, Security — several marked external), Company (About,
Customers, Careers) plus social icons and legal links (T&Cs, Privacy, Cookies).
The footer doubles as an SEO surface (glossary, CPV finder, competitors page,
tender explorer). Overall IA is product-led with solution/role segmentation in
dropdowns — heavier than a small-ICP product needs.

### Homepage structure

Very long page (11,718px at 1440w). Order: (1) announcement banner; (2) dark
near-black hero with large friendly rounded headline, accent-colored variable
phrase, and colorful abstract geometric shapes (their four-lobe logo motif
exploded into decorative fragments) framing the edges; (3) enterprise logo
marquee; (4) light section — two-sentence value statement + subhead covering
intelligence-to-bid-writing scope; (5) large product visual area (blank in
capture — lazy-loaded imagery); (6) pale-blue "built for every role" section
segmenting by persona; (7) eyebrow label "YOUR END-TO-END PLATFORM" +
"Predictable, scalable growth" headline introducing a 4-step feature
walkthrough that mirrors the funnel: find tenders/future opportunities (signal
scoring, one centralised feed) → create target account lists (buyer spend data)
→ bid/no-bid report triggering AI first draft → submit better bids faster
(Sales Studio + Bid Studio hand-off); each step has an icon, heading, body, and
a text link ("See how it works"/"Explore"); (8) Resources carousel (arrow
controls visible on mobile); (9) FAQ accordion (5 questions incl. cost, users,
integrations — a schema/objection-handling play); (10) boxed end-cap CTA card
with logo and "Get Started for Free"; (11) sitemap footer. CTA strategy is
dual-track throughout: free signup for self-serve, book-a-call for sales.
Caveat: several tall regions rendered blank in the capture (lazy-loaded product
screenshots/animations), so the page relies heavily on scroll-triggered product
imagery that this evidence cannot fully show.

### Conversion journey

Dual-path funnel: primary header CTA is sales-led "Book a call"; a free product
entry exists via "Search for tenders" (tool-as-lead-magnet) and "Get Started
for Free" in the end-cap and Free plan. Pricing page is refreshingly
transparent for the low tiers: four plans — Free (£0, tender tracking + pre-
tenders), Basic (from £99/mo, expiring contracts, live chat support), a
~£475/mo tier (buyer/supplier profiles, decision-maker contacts, framework
intelligence, FOI documents), and Expert (custom price, market analysis,
integrations, multi-team). "from £X /month" wording hedges actual cost
(inference: per-seat or annual-commitment variability). CTAs switch from self-
serve "Get started" (Free/Basic) to "Book a Call" (upper tiers) — a clear PLG-
to-sales-led seam at ~£475. Above the plan grid sits a product-suite selector
(Sales Studio / Bid Studio / B2G growth engine cards), adding a two-dimensional
choice (which studio × which tier) that increases cognitive load. Friction
points: cookie consent modal interrupts first paint on every page; no
annual/monthly toggle visible; no trial length stated in captures; FAQ carries
some pricing objection handling. Currency is GBP only — signals UK-first, a gap
for EUR-denominated EU buyers.

### Visual identity and brand personality

Character: friendly-confident scale-up, more playful than corporate. Type: a
rounded, geometric, slightly quirky display face for headlines (distinctive
single-story letterforms) paired with a plain neutral sans for body — the
headline face carries most of the brand personality. Color: royal blue primary
(buttons, links, logo tile), near-black hero panel, white and pale blue-grey
section grounds alternating for rhythm; a multi-color accent system (teal,
mustard/gold, purple, red) derived from the logo's four-lobe motif, used both
as decorative geometric hero shapes and as per-feature/per-product icon glyphs
— each product (Sales Studio, Bid Studio) gets its own shape-pair mark, a
scalable visual language for a product suite. Accent-colored words inside
headlines (gold, purple) highlight the variable/benefit phrase. Density: airy,
huge section paddings, generous line-height. Depth: soft-shadowed white cards
on light grounds; rounded corners everywhere (inputs, buttons, cards, banner).
Motion: not directly observable in stills, but the rotating hero phrase, blank
lazy-load regions, and resource-carousel arrows imply substantial scroll-
triggered animation and client-side rendering (inference). Giant wordmark
repeated in the footer as a brand sign-off.

### Product presentation

The captures show the skeleton of a heavily product-visual page — a large soft-
shadow card frame after the value-prop section and multiple tall reserved
regions — but the actual screenshots/animations did not render (lazy-loading
defeated the one-shot capture), so the product is effectively invisible in this
evidence. What is observable: each of the four platform-step sections pairs
copy with a (missing) visual and a deep-dive link, i.e. show-then-explain per
feature; the pricing page presents the product as three packaged "studios" with
icon marks; no interactive demo, sandbox, or video player chrome is visible;
the "Search for tenders" nav CTA and external "Tender explorer" link suggest a
public search tool acting as a live product demo (inference from labels).
Lesson and warning at once: a JS-animation-dependent page shows nothing when
scripts/lazy-load fail — bad for resilience, SEO rendering, and slow
connections.

### Mobile experience

At 390px the page holds up structurally: hamburger menu replaces the nav (logo

- burger only, no visible CTA in the collapsed header — a conversion miss on
  mobile); hero stacks with the geometric shapes reduced to a compact row above
  the headline; "Book a call" persists as the hero button; logo marquee becomes a
  horizontal scroller showing one logo at a time; feature sections stack cleanly
  with icon + heading + body + link at comfortable reading size; FAQ accordion
  and footer columns (two-up link columns, then Company) reflow well; the end-cap
  CTA card survives intact with "Get Started for Free". Same 11,718px total

  consultancies without procurement departments — also an SEO surface TED leaves
  open.
- Turn TED's authority into BidMorrow's trust layer: prominent, honest 'built on
  the official TED dataset' framing with the mandatory 2011/833/EU attribution —
  borrow the source-of-record credibility instead of competing with it.
- Design as the anti-TED without saying so: generous spacing, one clear
  search/qualification entry point, visible desktop navigation, restrained
  consent UX, and a coherent macOS-inspired visual system — every polish gap on
  TED is a felt pain BidMorrow's audience recognizes.
- Own the account-value story TED skips: make it obvious within seconds what
  saved profiles, matching, and alerts do — TED's unexplained 'Log in' shows how
  much value is lost by not narrating it.
- Publish an honest data-freshness signal (last-ingest time per OJ S issue) —
  echoing TED's issue-date card is the one pattern worth adapting, and it doubles
  as a truthful coverage statement.

### Design lessons (patterns described, never copied)

- The 'latest issue / next issue' currency card is the strongest element on the
  page: concrete dates prove the data is alive. A BidMorrow equivalent (last TED
  sync, notices ingested today) would be a cheap, truthful trust signal.
- Grouped link directories with clear headings (General information / Send
  notices / Reuse notices) are scannable even when ugly — grouping by user task
  beats grouping by org chart, and BidMorrow's footer should group by task the
  same way.
- The official-source verification strip shows how a single persistent line of
  provenance builds trust; BidMorrow can adapt the idea as a persistent,
  understated data-source attribution line.
- Negative lesson — hiding primary nav behind a hamburger on desktop measurably
  obscures the IA; keep desktop nav visible.
- Negative lesson — consent UX that overlaps content on both viewports poisons
  the first impression; a compliant but spatially contained consent pattern is a
  differentiator in this market.
- Negative lesson — offering multiple undifferentiated entry points
  (quick/advanced/expert) without guidance splits users; one primary action with
  progressive disclosure is better.
- Negative lesson — promoting video tutorials with a static cartoon banner
  instead of showing the product: always show the actual interface where
  comprehension matters.

## 7. Linear (linear.app) — indirect design reference

**Pages reviewed**: <https://linear.app/>

**One-line read**: A near-black, typography-led single-scroll narrative that sells a workflow
("intake → plan → build → diffs → monitor") through large annotated product
frames, numbered chapter links, and extreme restraint — the current benchmark
for premium SaaS marketing craft.

### Positioning and messaging

Category-creation positioning: the hero declares a category ("the product
development system") for a dual audience ("teams and agents"), followed by a
single one-line subhead framing purpose-built planning/building "for the AI
era". No feature list in the hero — the claim is identity, not capability.
Proof stack is threefold: (1) a large, richly detailed product frame directly
under the hero showing the app doing real-feeling work (issue thread, agent
panel, activity log); (2) an 8-logo customer strip (Vercel, Cursor, Oscar,
OpenAI, Coinbase, Cash App, Boom, Ramp) immediately below; (3) near the footer,
two named/attributed customer quotes with title+company plus a quantified
adoption stat ("powers over 40,000 product teams") linking to customer stories.
A giant mid-page manifesto paragraph in two-tone text ("A new species of
product tool…") restates positioning as thesis rather than benefits. Audience
is clearly product/engineering teams at software companies; every section
speaks their vocabulary (PRDs, PRs, diffs, triage, cycles).

### Sitemap and navigation

Very shallow top nav: 6 text items (Product, Resources, Customers, Pricing,
Now, Contact) + Log in + a pill "Sign up" button — 8 interactive elements
total, logo left, actions right, on a dark translucent bar. No mega-menu
visible in the static capture (Product/Resources are presumably dropdowns —
inference). The heavy IA lives in the footer: 5 columns (Product, Features,
Company, Resources, Connect) with ~30 links including Security, Documentation,
Enterprise, Startups, Brand, Method, plus a legal row (Privacy, Terms, DPA,
AUP). Notable pattern: each homepage section carries its own numbered sub-nav
("1.0 Intake →", "1.1 Linear Agent / 1.2 Triage / 1.3 Customer Requests / 1.4
Linear Asks", "2.1 Projects / 2.2 Documents…", up to "5.3 Dashboards") — the
homepage doubles as a table of contents for the feature architecture, so deep
pages are reachable without a fat header nav.

### Homepage structure

~10,900px at 1440w. Order: (1) Hero: two-line H1 top-left-aligned, one-line
subhead, and — unusually — no hero CTA button; instead a "New — Coding Sessions
→" announcement link sits right-aligned on the subhead row. (2) Full-width
product frame (~700px tall) showing a realistic issue + AI-agent side panel.
(3) Logo strip, 8 logos, one row. (4) Manifesto: two giant sentences, first
phrase bright white, remainder dimmed grey — a two-tone emphasis device. (5)
Three-up "principles" cards (Purpose-built / Powered by agents / Designed for
speed), each with a fine-line isometric 3D illustration labeled like a
technical figure ("FIG 0.2/0.3/0.4"). (6–10) Five feature chapters, identical
template: left large two-line H2, right a 3-line supporting paragraph, a
numbered link ("1.0 Intake →"), then a huge product screenshot composition,
then a numbered sub-link grid. Chapters: intake/triage, roadmap/planning,
agents, PR review/diffs, analytics dashboards. (11) Changelog: horizontal
timeline with 4 dated entry cards + "View all →" — shipping velocity as social
proof. (12) Two large quote cards (one lavender-tinted, one acid yellow — the
only saturated color on the page) with named attribution, then the 40,000-teams
stat line. (13) Final CTA: centered oversized two-line closer ("Built for the
future. / Available today." style: promise + availability), with the page's
first real buttons: "Get started" (light, primary) + "Contact sales" (dark
outline). (14) Footer. Argument order is thesis → proof → principles → workflow
story → momentum → testimony → ask: the CTA is earned at the end, not begged at
the top.

### Conversion journey

Remarkably low-pressure: the only persistent CTA is the nav "Sign up" pill; the
body contains zero buttons until the final section's Get started/Contact sales
pair (self-serve + enterprise fork in one row). All mid-page links are "learn
more" style arrows into feature pages, not signup pushes. Pricing is one click
from the nav (not shown in this capture). No banners, popups, cookie walls,
chat widgets, or exit intent visible in the rendered page. Inference: this
works because the brand has established demand; a page this CTA-sparse is risky
for an unknown product. Trial mechanics not observable from the homepage
capture.

### Visual identity and brand personality

Near-black ground (#0a0a0a-ish) throughout with subtle section-boundary tone
shifts; text in white → multiple precisely stepped greys (the dimming hierarchy
does most of the design work). One display sans (geometric grotesque) at
enormous sizes for H1/H2/manifesto; a monospace face for the numbering system
("1.0", "FIG 0.3", dates like "JUL 30, 2026"), giving a technical-
document/blueprint character. Color is rationed: interface screenshots supply
muted blues/greens/ambers; the only loud moments are the two quote cards (soft
lavender gradient, acid yellow) near the end — color as punctuation. Imagery is
entirely (a) real product UI frames with soft outer glow/rounded corners on the
dark ground and (b) fine-line wireframe isometric 3D objects as abstract
feature icons. Density is extremely low: 300–600px of pure black breathing room
between chapters. Motion is not capturable in a static PNG; the large empty
dark bands adjacent to some feature frames suggest scroll-triggered or video
content that hadn't animated in (inference). Overall character: austere,
confident, engineered — a spec sheet elevated to editorial.

### Product presentation

The product is the artwork: five full-width, high-fidelity app compositions,
each staged mid-task with a coherent fictional narrative (a Slack thread about
slow iOS startup becomes an issue, an agent picks it up, drafts a PR, it gets
reviewed, dashboards report on it). This continuity across sections — the same
story threading through intake, planning, agents, diffs, analytics — is the
page's most sophisticated device: screenshots read as one movie, not
disconnected features. Frames are cropped and layered (side panels overlapping
boards, a floating agent chat window over the issue view, a syntax-highlighted
red/green diff view), always partially bleeding off-frame to imply depth. No
stock imagery, no illustration-of-people, no video player chrome visible; no
interactive embedded demo in the static capture.

### Mobile experience

At 390px the page holds up structurally: nav collapses to logo + Log in + Sign
up pill + hamburger; H1 rewraps to five lines and stays left-aligned and
legible; the chapter template (H2, paragraph, numbered link) stacks cleanly
with typography scaled well. The hero product frame is bled off the right edge
(intentional crop) but its interior text is far below legible size — decorative
only. The logo strip and the quote cards run off-canvas to the right (yellow
card half-visible), implying horizontal scroll/marquee strips — acceptable but
scroll affordance is not visually signposted. Notable degradation: the mobile
page is 6,414px vs 10,898px desktop — several large product visuals appear as
empty black regions (e.g. above "Move work forward" and "Understand progress"),
and the Changelog timeline section is absent from the mobile capture,
suggesting media is dropped/lazy-loaded/unrendered on mobile (observation from
capture; could partly be a capture-timing artifact). Net: text architecture
survives perfectly; visual proof thins out considerably on mobile.

### Accessibility and usability observations

Concerns observable from the captures: heavy reliance on low-contrast dimmed
grey text on near-black (the manifesto's second half, section eyebrow numbers,
footer links, changelog dates all look near or below WCAG AA contrast —
inference from rendered pixels, not measured); meaning carried by brightness
difference alone in the two-tone manifesto; interface screenshots contain
unreadable micro-text (fine if properly alt-texted, unverifiable here);
horizontal-scrolling strips on mobile without visible affordance or controls;
the single-theme dark design offers no light alternative. Positives: generous
tap targets on the two real buttons, strong heading hierarchy, left-aligned
ragged text (no justified blocks), consistent focus-drawing layout rhythm, no
autoplaying carousels of content-critical text visible.

### SEO and content strategy

Homepage is thin on crawlable prose by design — big claims, short paragraphs;
the SEO weight clearly lives in the deep architecture the footer and numbered
links expose: per-feature pages (Intake, Plan, Build, Diffs, Monitor, plus
Asks/Agents/Coding Sessions/Integrations…), Customers, Blog, Method (their
famous practice-essays content moat), Documentation, Changelog, Switch
(competitor-migration pages), Startups/Enterprise segment pages. URL structure
not directly observable from screenshots beyond these labeled destinations. The
Changelog-on-homepage pattern doubles as freshness signal. Inference: their
durable organic asset is the "Method" essay content and docs, not the homepage.

### Trust, security, privacy presentation

Trust is argued through customers, not badges: 8 recognizable logos high on the
page, two named quotes with real names/titles/companies, and a quantified
adoption stat. Compliance surface is relegated to the footer: Security page
link under Product, plus Privacy/Terms/DPA/AUP legal row — a DPA link in the
footer signals EU/enterprise-data seriousness without homepage clutter. No
SOC2/ISO badge wall, no review-site widgets, no cookie banner visible in
capture. The realistic in-product data in screenshots (plausible names,
timestamps, issue IDs) itself functions as a credibility device.

### Strengths

- One continuous fictional workflow threads through every product screenshot,
  turning five feature sections into a single narrative demo
- Typography does the branding: two typefaces, a strict grey scale, and rationed
  color produce a distinctive identity with almost no decorative assets
- Numbered chapter/sub-link system (1.0–5.3) makes the homepage a legible table
  of contents into deep feature pages
- Extreme restraint in conversion pressure — zero body CTAs until the earned
  final ask — reads as confidence and keeps the narrative unbroken
- Changelog-as-social-proof section demonstrates shipping velocity with dated
  entries instead of adjectives
- Fine-line 'FIG 0.x' isometric illustrations give abstract principles a
  consistent technical-blueprint character
- Mobile typography and section rhythm survive intact; the text architecture is
  genuinely responsive
- Named, attributed quotes plus a hard adoption number instead of anonymous
  testimonial mush

### Weaknesses

- Large parts of the dimmed-grey text hierarchy look at/below AA contrast on
  near-black; the brand aesthetic is bought with legibility (inference from
  pixels, unmeasured)
- No hero CTA at all: a first-time visitor who is convinced early must scroll
  ~10,000px or find the small nav pill — only works with pre-existing brand
  demand
- Mobile loses substantial proof: several product visuals render as empty black
  bands and the Changelog section is missing at 390px, so the strongest evidence
  thins where most first visits happen
- Horizontal-scroll logo and quote strips on mobile have no visible affordance or
  controls
- Single dark theme with no light option; long dark pages can fatigue and
  print/preview poorly
- The homepage assumes deep insider vocabulary (PRDs, diffs, triage, cycles) —
  zero on-ramp for buyers outside the tribe; fine for Linear, fatal for a
  category the buyer doesn't already understand
- Screenshot micro-text is illegible everywhere it appears; the product is shown
  but never actually readable
- Almost no crawlable substantive copy on the page itself — the homepage rides on
  brand search, delegating SEO entirely to deep pages

### Differentiation opportunities for BidMorrow

- Adopt the 'one story through every section' device with procurement substance:
  a single realistic tender travels the page — ingested from TED, scored,
  explained, dismissed-or-pursued — but make the frames LEGIBLE at rendered size
  (readable score rationale beats Linear's decorative micro-text) and truthful to
  the deterministic engine
- Invert the audience assumption: Linear can afford insider jargon; BidMorrow's
  5–50-person consultancy buyer needs the category explained — pair every chapter
  with a concrete outcome sentence (hours not wasted, deadlines not missed)
  rather than a manifesto
- Keep Linear's CTA restraint as rhythm but not its absence: BidMorrow has no
  brand demand, so place one quiet, consistent primary action in the hero and
  repeat it at the close — earned tone, findable button
- Steal the numbered-chapter navigation idea as a scannable 'how qualification
  works' spine (signal → score → evidence → decision), which also suits
  BidMorrow's honesty rules: each number can link to a truthful methodology page
  instead of marketing fluff
- Do dark-mode discipline without the contrast debt: if using a dim hierarchy,
  verify AA on every grey step — an accessibility-clean version of this aesthetic
  is itself a differentiator, and BidMorrow's CSP/no-third-party stance can be
  marketed as part of the trust story Linear buries in the footer
- Linear proves velocity with a changelog; BidMorrow can prove trustworthiness
  with a visible data-honesty block (TED attribution, coverage statement,
  decision-support disclaimer) styled with the same 'FIG/spec-sheet' dignity
  instead of legal small print
- Their mobile drops evidence; BidMorrow should make the mobile page carry full
  proof — a lightweight, static, CSP-safe scored-tender card renders fine at
  390px where Linear's heavy media fails
- Their social proof is logos and quotes BidMorrow cannot truthfully use pre-
  launch; substitute verifiable mechanics (real scoring criteria, real TED notice
  structure, founding-50 honesty) as the proof layer — specificity as the
  credibility device

### Design lessons (patterns described, never copied)

- Two-tone sentence emphasis: set a thesis paragraph huge, first clause bright,
  remainder dimmed — hierarchy inside a single sentence without bolding or color
- Chapter template discipline: identical layout per feature section (left H2,
  right 3-line paragraph, numbered arrow link, full-width visual, sub-link grid)
  creates rhythm that makes a very long page feel organized
- Ration color to one or two moments: on a monochrome page, two saturated quote
  cards near the end land like a chorus — reserve accent color for the emotional
  beat, not for every button
- Monospace as a labeling voice: use a mono face only for numbers, dates, and
  figure labels to inject technical credibility without theming the whole page
- Product frames bleeding off-canvas with soft glow and rounded corners imply a
  larger living product; partial crops are more evocative than complete window
  shots
- Whitespace as pacing: 300–600px of empty ground between chapters forces one
  idea per viewport and makes each visual feel like an exhibit
- End-of-page CTA choreography: oversized two-line closing statement (promise +
  availability) directly above the primary/secondary button pair — the entire
  page is the argument, the last viewport is the ask
- Footer as the real sitemap: keep the header to ~6 items and let a 5-column
  footer carry the full IA including legal/DPA/security links
- Consistent fictional data across screenshots (same names, ticket IDs,
  timestamps) makes mockups feel like documentary evidence — worth replicating
  with sanitized realistic TED notice data
- Numbered sub-link grids under each section ('1.1…1.4') turn marketing sections
  into navigable documentation entry points
