Stratum II

Qualify — your profile brings one notice into focus

Each eligible notice is scored 0–100 against your company profile by a deterministic engine. No black box, no “AI magic” — the same notice, the same profile, and the same engine version always produce the same score.

Every component is inspectable. When a data point is missing from the published notice, BidMorrow never guesses and never scores it as zero — it applies a documented neutral score, half of that component’s maximum, and marks the component Unknown with an explanation of what was missing.

Risk flags quote the exact source text they were detected from and are labelled either “Confirmed pattern” or “Possible requirement detected — verify in source documents.” BidMorrow never asserts a requirement exists without quoting the evidence it found.

How it works

From official journal to defensible decision

Four steps, no black box. The order matters: the source is official, the profile is yours, the scoring is deterministic, and the verdict is explained.

  1. TED, ingested daily

    Competition notices from Tenders Electronic Daily — the Publications Office of the European Union — within a documented CPV scope.

  2. Your company profile

    CPV codes, capabilities, keywords, geography, value range, exclusions — set once in onboarding, editable forever after.

  3. Deterministic scoring

    Same input, same engine version, same score — every time. Eight inspectable components, 0–100. No LLM anywhere in the scoring path.

  4. Verdict + explanation

    Strong match, worth reviewing, or possible — with the reasoning for every point, and a link to the original notice on TED.

Methodology

Deterministic on purpose

The category sells “AI-powered” magic. A bid/no-bid call you might defend to a managing partner deserves better: a scoring engine you can audit, rerun, and disagree with — point by point.

Unknowns are never guessed

When a notice omits a value or a deadline, the affected component gets a documented neutral score — half its maximum — and is marked Unknown in the breakdown, with an explanation of what was missing. Never zero, never a silent perfect match.

Contract value · not published → 5.0 / 10 · marked “Unknown”

Risk flags quote their evidence

Certifications, clearances, turnover thresholds, framework membership — detected by deterministic pattern matches over the notice text, never an LLM guess. Each flag quotes the exact source text it was found in.

“…certificering conform ISO/IEC 27001 is vereist…” → Confirmed pattern · ISO 27001 (sample)

FAQ

Fair questions, straight answers

Where does the data come from, and how fresh is it?

Every notice comes from Tenders Electronic Daily (TED), the official journal of EU public procurement, published by the Publications Office of the European Union. BidMorrow ingests competition notices daily within its documented CPV scope, and every match links back to the original notice on TED so you can read the source documents.

Is BidMorrow AI-powered?

No — deliberately. Scoring is deterministic and LLM-free: the same notice, the same profile, and the same engine version always produce the same score, and every component of every score is inspectable. When data is missing from a notice, we apply a documented neutral score and label the component “Unknown” — we never guess.

What does “scoped coverage” actually mean?

BidMorrow ingests TED competition notices within a documented CPV scope — currently IT services (CPV 72*), software packages and information systems (CPV 48*), and a small reviewed extras list (safety consultancy, CPV 79417000). A notice outside this scope is never ingested or scored, regardless of how well it might otherwise fit your profile. Before scoring, a CPV pre-filter also requires a shared CPV division with your preferences — so keep your CPV list honest and revisit it if your feed seems too quiet.

What are the billing terms?

Monthly subscription via Stripe — cancel any time from account settings. No annual contract, no usage-based fees, no hidden tiers. Founding pricing (€29/month) is limited to the first 20 customers and may not be available when you sign up; once you’re on the founding price, it’s retained for the life of your subscription.
